Every festive season brings a wave of "recycled plastic" claims on gift packaging, and most buyers nod along without asking what backs them up. This year, that question has a sharper answer than usual, and it isn't reassuring.

## The scale this season

A Redseer report cited by The Week projects online retail growing 25% in the 30 to 35 days leading up to Diwali this year, up from 16% last year, after what the report calls the strongest first half for online retail in four years. That means more parcels moving through courier networks this month than in any festive season on record, each one wrapped in something, often something claiming to be recycled plastic.

## What the numbers actually show

Sunita Narain, who heads the Centre for Science and Environment and edits Down To Earth, laid out the problem in a column published this September. India's Extended Producer Responsibility system reports 17 million tonnes of plastic packaging recycled since 2022, a trade she values at roughly Rs 2,500 crore. Behind that figure sit 41,544 producers, importers and brand owners registered in the system, reporting their own numbers. The Central Pollution Control Board's own estimate puts the real cost of recycling category 2 plastic at Rs 4,000 to Rs 6,000 a tonne. Recyclers are credited far less, around Rs 1,500 a tonne for flexible plastic. Someone is absorbing that gap, or the number being reported isn't quite what it claims to be.

The part that matters most for anyone trying to check this themselves: as of 28 June 2026, the EPR portal holding this data is open only to what Narain calls "a closed group of users." Not the public. Not fully to researchers. She also points to a 2024 analysis by her own CSE colleagues of Agra's drains, where branded plastic packaging made up 70% of what they pulled out. Recycling claims and visible reality don't always meet in the middle.

## How this connects to the audit rules

We [wrote last month](/blogs/what-indias-2026-plastic-rules-mean-for-corporate-gifting/) about the 2026 amendment that replaces self-declared EPR compliance with independent audits, a direct response to CPCB's own 2023 disclosure of roughly 700,000 fake compliance certificates. That audit requirement is a real fix, on paper. But an audit is only as good as the data it's checking, and Narain's column says that data still isn't open enough to verify from the outside. The rule changed. Access didn't, not yet.

## What this means if you're ordering gifts right now

None of this means every "recycled" claim on a gift box is false. It means you, as a buyer, have no practical way to check one before Diwali, a little over a month away now. So don't try. Ask a different question instead: was this ever plastic-packaged to begin with? A hamper boxed in cloth, a pencil wrapped in a kraft sleeve, a notebook with a seed-paper cover, none of that runs through the EPR credit system at all, because there's nothing plastic to report. It sidesteps the entire self-reporting question, portal access included.
