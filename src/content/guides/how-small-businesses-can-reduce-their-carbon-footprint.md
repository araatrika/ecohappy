A carbon footprint is the total greenhouse gas output tied to a person's, organisation's, or product's activity: transport, energy use, and the goods it buys and makes. For a small business, most of that footprint sits in three places: sourcing, packaging, and shipping. Those also happen to be the three easiest to change without touching the core business.

## What we actually changed at Eco Happy

Rather than list generic advice, here's what this looked like for us specifically.

We swapped standard wood pencils for ones rolled from old newspaper, and moved several product lines to seed paper that grows into herbs or flowers after use instead of going to landfill. Neither required new machinery or a different supplier network. Just a different raw material.

Packaging went from mixed plastic to kraft paper and minimal filler. It's a small change per order, but it adds up fast at any real order volume, since packaging waste is one of the few footprint sources a business controls entirely on its own.

For bulk and corporate orders, several of our lines, the 100-unit seed pen and newspaper pencil batches, for instance, are made to order instead of held as standing inventory. We only make what's actually been ordered, which means no unsold stock quietly going to waste.

## The same principles apply beyond stationery

Source ethically, and know where your materials actually come from. Buy locally where you can, since transport is one of the more overlooked emissions sources for a small business. Go digital for anything that doesn't need to be printed. Replace single-use plastics with reusable or biodegradable alternatives, even in small internal things like office supplies. And use recyclable outer packaging by default, not as an occasional upgrade.

None of this requires a sustainability department or a large budget. It requires picking the consumables you already buy every month and choosing the version that doesn't need to be thrown away.
