Diwali is a little over four weeks away, and gift and hamper shopping is already picking up. Most people pick by how a hamper looks in photos, and most procurement teams pick by price per unit. Neither group usually asks what happens to the box after it's opened. This year there's a sharper reason to ask anyway.

## What one gift wrapper adds up to

Take Nagpur. After Diwali in 2024, the city's sanitation department told local reporters that waste collection jumped by more than 200 tonnes in just two days, against a typical load of about 77 tonnes a day from markets and 64 tonnes from street cleaning. That's not a one-off. It's what happens when a few days of concentrated gift-giving, wrapping paper, foil, and packaging meet a city's normal collection capacity.

Zoom out and the pattern holds nationally. A February 2026 NITI Aayog sectoral report puts India's municipal solid waste at roughly 101 million tonnes a year, with 61% of it ending up in landfill rather than composted or recycled. A festival that concentrates a chunk of the country's annual gift-giving into about a week doesn't create that number by itself. But it adds a visible spike on top of it, in the one week it's hardest for a sanitation crew to miss.

## Why the math changes for a corporate order

One gift wrapped in plastic film is a small decision. Five hundred identical ones, ordered for employees or clients and wrapped the same way, is a different decision entirely, just repeated at scale. The packaging rules we've [written about before](/blogs/what-indias-2026-plastic-rules-mean-for-corporate-gifting/) apply to exactly this kind of volume: producers, importers and brand owners of plastic packaging now sit inside an EPR compliance chain, and a procurement team ordering branded plastic hampers by the hundred is buying into that chain whether or not anyone mentions it at checkout.

There's also a slower structural shift happening above procurement's head. SEBI has required India's 1,000 largest listed companies by market capitalisation to file an annual Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report since FY 2022-23. Separately, under Section 135 of the Companies Act, any company crossing Rs 500 crore net worth, Rs 1,000 crore turnover, or Rs 5 crore net profit has to spend at least 2% of its average net profit on CSR. Neither rule mentions gift hampers by name. But both mean more companies than ever have someone whose job includes asking where the budget and the packaging ended up, and a Diwali gifting line item doesn't get a quiet pass just because it's small.

## What "eco-friendly" should mean on a hamper

"Eco-friendly" on a hamper should name an actual material, seed paper, kraft, cloth or steel, not just sit as a word on a box wrapped in cellophane with a leaf printed on it. The packaging has to match that claim, and ideally you can ask who made the thing and get a real answer back. We've gone deeper on spotting the difference between a real claim and a decorated one in an [earlier guide](/blogs/what-are-sustainable-gifts-why-they-matter-more-than-ever/), if you want the longer version.
